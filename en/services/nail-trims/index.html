Nail trims for dogs and cats

When Are Nails Too Long?

If you can hear your dog’s nails clicking on a hard floor, or if they touch the floor when your dog stands still, they are probably too long. Long nails can change how a dog stands and walks, and they catch and split more easily. Dewclaws, the nails on the inner side of the leg, do not touch the ground and do not wear down, so they can curl round into the pad. Older cats’ claws can grow thick and long and curl into the pad too, so check them regularly. If a nail or claw has already grown into the pad, don’t try to pull it out yourself: call us.

Trimming at Home

Use sharp clippers made for pets and trim only the tip, a little at a time. Inside the nail is the quick, which contains blood vessels and nerves: cutting into it hurts and bleeds. In light nails you can see the pink quick; in dark nails, cut thin slices and stop when a dark spot appears in the middle of the cut end. Get your pet used to having its paws handled, with treats, ideally from a young age. If a nail bleeds, press a clean cloth firmly on it for five minutes without lifting it to look. If it is still bleeding after that, keep pressing and call us; outside opening hours, call the regional emergency vet on 0600 399 299.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a nail trim cost? €20 if you hold your pet, €30 if a nurse holds it and €40 if two or more nurses are needed. These are total prices.

How often should a dog’s nails be trimmed? When they click on a hard floor or touch the floor while your dog stands still. Many dogs need a trim once or twice a month. Walking on hard surfaces helps wear nails down but may not be enough, and dewclaws don’t wear down at all.

What if I cut a nail too short? Press a clean cloth firmly on it for five minutes without lifting it. If it is still bleeding, keep pressing and call us, or the regional emergency vet outside opening hours.
