Microchipping for dogs and cats

What Is a Microchip?

A microchip is about the size of a grain of rice. It goes under the skin, usually between the shoulder blades, and carries a unique 15-digit number. It has no battery and usually lasts for life. A chip is not a tracker: it cannot show where your pet is. It only gives its number when it is scanned, and for dogs, Ruokavirasto’s dog register links that number to you.

Dogs Must Be Chipped and Registered

Every dog living in Finland must be microchipped and registered in Ruokavirasto’s dog register. A puppy must be chipped and registered by the age of three months, or before it moves to a new home if that comes sooner. When you get a puppy, check that it is already chipped and registered. A dog moving to Finland from abroad must arrive chipped and be registered within four weeks of arrival. We give you the chip number in writing, and you then register your dog yourself at koirarekisteri.ruokavirasto.fi, which costs €10 online. Report changes to the register within a month: a new owner (both the old and the new owner report it), new contact details, or your dog’s death or disappearance. If a dog is found unchipped or unregistered, the owner is told to put it right. Serious neglect can also bring a penalty fee of €300 or more. Rules checked in November 2026.

Cats

In Finland, a pet cat needs a chip only if it travels abroad. There is no official cat register yet; Ruokavirasto expects to open one in 2030. Under new EU rules, from 31 August 2030 a cat must be chipped and registered before it is sold or given away; an occasional private gift made without an online advert is exempt. We recommend chipping now: it gives your cat a permanent ID, and the chip will still meet the new rules as long as it can be read.

Travel and Kennel Club Registration

A dog or cat travelling within the EU needs a microchip, a rabies vaccination and an EU pet passport. Dogs entering Finland, including on the way home, usually need a tapeworm treatment too. Puppies must be chipped before the breeder registers them with the Finnish Kennel Club; we can chip them at the puppy check-up. Kennel Club registration does not replace Ruokavirasto’s dog register.

Microchipping at Our Clinic

A vet chips your pet while it is awake. The chip is injected under the skin in a moment, and no sedation is needed. We scan first to check your pet is not already chipped, and again afterwards to make sure the new chip reads. You get the chip number in writing, and we save it in our records. Microchipping costs €68 on its own (visit fee included), or €32.40 with another visit, such as a vaccination. Book online under ‘Rokotus, passi ja matkustus → Mikrosirun laitto’ or call us on (06) 321 7300.

Free Chip Reading

Want to check that your pet’s chip still works, or have you found a dog or cat? Come in during opening hours: we read chips free of charge, and no appointment is needed. For a found animal, also call the lost-animal shelter. For Vaasa, Mustasaari, Vöyri and Maalahti, that is Koiratarha Kulkuri, 040 834 2222.

Frequently asked questions about microchipping

How much does microchipping cost? €68 on its own (visit fee included), or €32.40 with another visit, such as a vaccination.

Does microchipping hurt? The chip is injected under the skin, which takes only a moment. Your pet stays awake, and no sedation is needed.

Can a microchip show where my pet is? No. A chip has no GPS or battery. It only gives its number when scanned, and for dogs, the dog register links that number to you.
