Stool sample test for dogs and cats

When Is a Stool Sample Useful?

A stool sample can reveal worms and other gut parasites, such as Giardia, but a negative result does not always mean there are none. A healthy, low-risk adult pet can be tested instead of routine deworming, if tested as often as it would be dewormed. A test also helps when diarrhoea lasts or keeps coming back, for a new pet from a shelter or abroad, and for a dog with a long-lasting cough, as some lungworms show up in stool. Stool tests miss many tapeworms, so pets that hunt, or eat raw meat not first frozen for a week, still need regular tapeworm treatment.

How to Collect the Sample

If your dog coughs or your pet is clearly unwell, call us first, as a different sample or a visit may be needed. From a day or two before collecting until you finish, keep your dog on a lead and your cat indoors: eaten droppings or prey can give a false result. Many parasites pass eggs, larvae or cysts unevenly — some days none — so collect stool on three days in a row into one clean, tightly closed container, about a tablespoonful each day. Pick it up right after your pet has gone, with as little grass, soil or cat litter as possible. Keep it in the fridge, not the freezer, and write your pet’s name on it.

Bringing the Sample to Us

Bring the sample to our reception soon after the last day, during opening hours; no appointment is needed. The vet chooses the test with you. The sample goes to an outside laboratory, and results take about a week. A vet then contacts you with the result and any advice.

Price

The price depends on the test and is set mainly by the laboratory, so it can change. Examples from autumn 2026, test only, including handling, postage and laboratory fees: worm-egg check €107.97; wider parasite test including Giardia €160.00.
