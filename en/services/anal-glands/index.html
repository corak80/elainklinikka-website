Anal gland problems in dogs and cats

What Are Anal Glands?

Dogs and cats have two small sacs, the anal glands, one on each side of the anus. They hold a strong-smelling fluid that is normally squeezed out when the animal passes stool. If the fluid does not empty, the sacs fill up and can become inflamed or form an abscess. This is common in dogs and much rarer in cats.

Signs

Scooting the bottom along the floor, licking or biting under the tail, a fishy smell, or pain when sitting down or passing stool. Scooting can also have other causes, such as itchy skin from an allergy, worms or dirt stuck around the bottom.

When to Call

Call us the same day if there is a red, painful swelling next to the anus, blood or pus, or if your pet is in pain, dull or off its food: these can be signs of an abscess. Outside opening hours, call the regional emergency vet on 0600 399 299. Have any lump next to the anus checked, even if it does not hurt. Don’t squeeze the glands or a swelling yourself: squeezing them the wrong way can make them burst.

Emptying at the Clinic

A nurse empties the glands in a 15-minute appointment for €21.97 (total price). Book online — ‘Hoitaja-ajat → Anaalirauhasten tyhjennys (eläinhoitaja)’ — or call us on (06) 321 7300. If the glands are infected, painful or can’t be emptied, your pet needs to see a vet; sometimes the glands need to be flushed under sedation.

If It Keeps Coming Back

Some dogs need their glands emptied regularly. Common reasons are soft stools, being overweight and itchy skin from an allergy. Firm stools and a healthy weight help the glands empty, so ask us about diet. If your pet’s glands are fine, there is no need to have them emptied: emptying them too often or the wrong way can cause problems.
