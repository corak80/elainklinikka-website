Official Examinations

Official radiographic examinations

We take official hip, elbow and spine X-rays for the Finnish Kennel Club. As the Kennel Club’s instructions require, the dog is sedated so that it is relaxed enough for the correct X-ray positions. We send the X-rays electronically to the Kennel Club, whose evaluating vets grade them, usually within about a week, and the results are recorded in the Kennel Club’s breeding database (KoiraNet).

Patella and cardiac examinations

Official patella examinations are done without sedation: the vet feels both knees by hand to check that the kneecaps stay in place. Official heart auscultation, which listens for a heart murmur, is done by Assaf Wydra or Leena Sandström, and official heart ultrasound examinations by Leena Sandström; all our patella and heart examinations are done by vets on the Kennel Club’s lists of approved vets for them (read more on our cardiac examinations page). In a few breeds, such as the Dobermann and the Great Dane, auscultation alone is not recorded, and the official heart examination always includes ultrasound and ECG.

Who needs official examinations?

Official examinations are required or recommended for breeding dogs, depending on the breed. Their aim is to reduce hereditary diseases within breeds. Most official examinations can be done from 12 months of age, but some breeds and examinations have other age limits and a few have none, and how long a result stays valid depends on the examination and the breed — see the questions below.

PEVISA — which examinations does your breed need?

PEVISA is the Finnish Kennel Club’s breed-specific programme for combating hereditary diseases and defects; over 200 breeds and breed varieties have one. In these breeds the parents must have the required official examination results before mating: the results must be valid on the day of mating and within the programme’s limits, and some programmes add other conditions, such as a DNA test. If a result that was missing or out of date at mating is obtained before the litter is registered and meets the requirements, the litter is registered with an extra fee. Otherwise, unless an exemption was applied for and granted in advance, the puppies are registered with a permanent breeding ban and an extra fee, and a breeder can register only one such litter in five years; in a few breeds, puppies from certain results or combinations cannot be registered at all. A breeder can register at most three litters with an extra fee. The requirements differ from breed to breed, so check your breed’s page on the Finnish Kennel Club’s website and ask your breed club. We do the official hip, elbow and spine X-rays, patella examinations, heart auscultation and heart ultrasound. Official eye examinations are done by eye vets approved by the Kennel Club; we do not do them. Rules checked in November 2026.

Booking and information

Please book official examinations in advance. Before the visit, order the electronic referral for each examination in the Kennel Club’s Omakoira service; without it, the result cannot be recorded. Your dog must be identified, usually with a microchip, and we check the identification at the examination. X-ray examinations are done under sedation, so fast your dog as before anaesthesia (8–12 hours; water until the morning) and allow more time for the visit. We are happy to advise on which examinations are recommended for your dog’s breed. You can find prices for all official examinations in our price list.

Frequently asked questions about official examinations

How much do hip X-rays cost? Hip X-rays cost €183 for a dog under 20 kg, €193 for 20–40 kg and €233 over 40 kg. Elbow X-rays at the same visit cost €63 more.

At what age are official examinations performed? Hip and elbow X-rays can be taken from 12 months of age; in some breeds the PEVISA programme sets the limit at 18 months. In the spine X-ray, transitional vertebra (LTV) and vertebral anomalies (VA) are assessed from 12 months, and spondylosis from 24 months, as are calcified discs (IDD), which are assessed only in certain breeds. Patella examinations and official heart auscultation can be done from 12 months. For the official heart ultrasound, the limit is 24 months for acquired heart disease, with no lower limit for congenital heart disease. Some breeds’ PEVISA programmes set different limits, so check your breed’s requirements.

How long are results valid? X-ray results do not expire, but a dog can be X-rayed again after a set interval, and the new result may change the old one; some spine results can be given only once. A patella result given before three years of age is valid for two years; one given at three years or older does not expire. A heart auscultation result with no murmur, and an ultrasound result with no acquired heart disease, are valid for 12 months; an ultrasound result with no congenital heart disease given at 12 months or older does not need renewing. A breed’s PEVISA programme can set its own validity periods. Results are recorded in the Kennel Club’s breeding database (KoiraNet), where anyone can see them.
