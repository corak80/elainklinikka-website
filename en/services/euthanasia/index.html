Pet Euthanasia in Vaasa

When is it time?

Letting a pet go is never easy. It may be time when treatment no longer helps or your pet’s quality of life has fallen too low: pain that cannot be eased, eating very little, struggling to move, more bad days than good. If you are unsure, we will give you our honest view and decide with you.

What happens

First a sedative puts your pet into a deep sleep, usually within a few minutes. You can stay throughout, or say goodbye before the sedation; both are fine. The vet then gives the final injection, usually into a vein, where it brings full unconsciousness within seconds. Your pet does not suffer; the heart stops within minutes, and the vet checks to confirm it. The eyes often stay open, and the body may twitch, seem to take a breath or empty the bladder or bowels; your pet is not aware of this.

At the clinic

Euthanasia is done only at our clinic; we do not make home visits. Call (06) 321 7300 and tell us it is for euthanasia, so we can book a calm, unhurried time. You are welcome to bring your pet’s favourite blanket or toy. If your pet is suffering outside our opening hours (Mon–Fri 7:45–17:00), call the regional emergency vet on 0600 399 299.

Cremation or burial

We can arrange cremation (shared, or individual with the ashes returned to you), or you can bury your pet on your own land. The Finnish Food Authority (Ruokavirasto) says the grave should be at least a metre deep and filled in straight away, with no plastic or other wrapping that does not rot. It recommends avoiding groundwater areas, water-intake protection zones, slopes down to water and anywhere within 250 metres of a well.

Prices

Cat €128. Dog under 20 kg €143, 20–40 kg €173, over 40 kg €193. Rodents and rabbits €88. These are total prices, including the visit fee and sedation; cremation is extra.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to put a cat or dog to sleep? A cat €128 and a dog €143–193 by weight, visit fee and sedation included; cremation is extra. Can euthanasia be done at home? Not by us: only at our clinic. Can a vet refuse euthanasia? We never refuse when an animal is suffering. A vet may decline to put down a healthy animal; we then talk through other options, such as rehoming.

Grief

Grief for a pet is real and takes its own time. Let children say goodbye and grieve too, and use a plain word like ‘died’: ‘put to sleep’ can frighten young children. If you want to talk, we are here.
