Pregnancy Scan for Dogs and Cats

Pregnancy ultrasound: when to book

In dogs, ultrasound can show a pregnancy from about day 25 after mating, but it is most reliable at 4–5 weeks, when the puppies’ heartbeats can usually be seen. Mating day is only a rough guide, because sperm can survive in the bitch for a week or more. Scan too early and a pregnancy can be missed, so we may ask you to come back. In cats, the best time is 3–4 weeks after mating. The scan uses no radiation and needs no sedation.

X-ray: counting the litter

The skeletons show on X-ray from about day 45 after mating in dogs and day 40 in cats. The count is most reliable late: in dogs from about day 55. In a large litter the skeletons can overlap and a puppy can be missed. The count helps you judge when the birth is over. Not sure whether one is left? Call us: an X-ray can check.

Late pregnancy and birth

Near the birth, ultrasound can check the puppies’ or kittens’ heart rates. A rate that stays low is a sign of distress and may mean a caesarean is needed. In Boston Terriers, English Bulldogs and French Bulldogs, most litters are born by caesarean, so plan ahead with us. We do planned caesareans and may also be able to help with urgent ones during opening hours. Call us at once if the mother strains for 30 minutes without a puppy or kitten, goes 2 hours between births while more are expected (even if she is resting), has no puppy 24 hours after her temperature drops, has dark green or foul-smelling discharge before the first birth, or seems unwell or bleeds heavily. Outside opening hours, call the regional emergency vet on 0600 399 299.

Frequently asked questions

When should I book the ultrasound? Dogs 4–5 weeks after mating, cats 3–4 weeks. Tell us all mating dates and any ovulation test results. Can you tell how many puppies or kittens there are? Only roughly by ultrasound. An X-ray late in pregnancy gives the most reliable count. Is X-ray safe in pregnancy? Late in pregnancy, once the organs have formed, it is considered very low risk.
