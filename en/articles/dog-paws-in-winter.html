Dog Paws in Winter: Salt, Snowballs and Frost

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

Road salt, snowballs and frost are tough on a dog’s paws in winter; a quick check after each walk prevents many problems.

Salt and grit

Salt can sting and crack the pads, and licking salty paws can upset a dog’s stomach. In Finland, salt is used mainly on busy roads; footpaths usually get sand or gravel, which can lodge between the toes. After walks near salted roads, rinse the paws in lukewarm water and dry them well, between the toes too.

Snowballs and cracked pads

Snow freezes into painful balls in long fur between the toes. Trim tufts that stick out past the pads with round-tipped scissors, or ask a groomer, but don’t cut deep between the pads. Melt ice balls in lukewarm water; don’t pull them. Paw balm for dogs may help protect dry pads but doesn’t replace rinsing. Well-fitting dog boots keep salt and snow off; get your dog used to them indoors first.

Frost and ice

Small, short-coated, young, old and ill dogs chill fastest, especially in wind or with wet fur: give them a coat and shorter walks in hard frost. If your dog shivers, hunches up, keeps lifting its paws or won’t walk on, head home. Frostbite most often hits the paws, ear tips, tail tip and scrotum. The skin turns pale or greyish, cold and hard, and later may redden, swell, blister or turn black; full damage can take days to show. Once indoors, where it can’t refreeze, gently warm the area in warm, not hot, water or with warm, wet towels. Don’t rub it or use a hairdryer. Thawing hurts; even a gentle dog may bite. Keep your dog on a lead near frozen water. Ice is never safe near harbours or shipping lanes, and always risky by bridges or flowing water. If your dog falls through, don’t follow it onto the ice: call 112. Once it’s out, dry it, keep it warm and have a vet check it.

Antifreeze

Antifreeze (car coolant) tastes sweet and is deadly: a few teaspoons of the undiluted liquid can kill a small dog. Wipe up spills. If your dog may have lapped any, call a vet at once, even if it seems well. Don’t make it vomit unless a vet says so (see our poisoning article).

When to see the vet

See your vet if your dog limps (see our limping article), a pad is cut or bleeding, a crack is deep or doesn’t heal in a few days, the paw swells, the skin between the toes stays red, or your dog keeps licking its paws. Call at once if you suspect frostbite or antifreeze poisoning, or your dog is weak or confused after time in the cold. Call us on (06) 321 7300. In an emergency out of hours, call the regional emergency vet on 0600 399 299.
