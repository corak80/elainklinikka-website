Diarrhoea in Dogs, Cats and Kittens — Home Care and When to Call

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

Most diarrhoea in dogs and cats settles within a few days, but it can be a sign of serious illness.

When to Call

Call us on (06) 321 7300 at once if your pet may have eaten something poisonous or swallowed an object, or if a male cat is straining in the litter tray: a blocked bladder is an emergency. Call the same day if there is blood in the stool (red, or black and tarry); your pet vomits repeatedly, is dull or weak, has a painful belly, dry gums or sunken eyes, or has refused food for a day; or your pet is elderly, has a long-term illness or takes medicine from a vet — if it is pain relief, give no more until you have spoken to us. Outside opening hours, call the regional emergency vet on 0600 399 299.

Kittens and Puppies

Diarrhoea is common in kittens after the move to a new home, a change of food or because of parasites. Kittens and puppies dehydrate quickly: call the same day if the young animal is dull, stops eating, vomits, or has watery or bloody diarrhoea — especially if it is not fully vaccinated, as panleukopenia and parvovirus can be life-threatening. If a lively kitten or puppy has soft stools, keep feeding small, frequent meals of its usual food or an easily digestible kitten or puppy food. Book a visit if it has not settled within a day or two, and bring a fresh stool sample: we send it to a laboratory to check for parasites such as Giardia. A pharmacy dewormer does not treat every cause.

Home Care for Adults

If your adult pet is otherwise healthy and bright, you can try home care for 24–48 hours. Don’t withhold food: give small, frequent meals of a bland, easily digestible food, nothing fatty. We sell easily digestible diets; boiled chicken and rice also works for a few days. Cats must keep eating: a few days of eating little or nothing can cause a dangerous liver disease. Provide fresh water, don’t give human medicines (some are poisonous to pets), and wash your hands after cleaning up. A probiotic made for dogs or cats may shorten diarrhoea a little, but the evidence is weak — ask us which one. Once the stool is normal, change back to the usual food over a few days. Book a visit if your pet is no better after two days, or the diarrhoea keeps coming back.

Common Causes

Eating rubbish or table scraps, a sudden change of food, parasites, stress, food allergy, some medicines, poisons, swallowed objects, infections, and illness elsewhere in the body. Diarrhoea that lasts more than about three weeks or keeps coming back needs its cause found.

Prevention

Change food gradually over about a week, keep your pet away from scraps and rubbish, deworm kittens and puppies regularly and adults according to their risk, and keep vaccinations up to date.
