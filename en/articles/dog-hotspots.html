Hotspots in Dogs — Signs, First Aid and When to See the Vet

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

A hotspot is a red, wet and painful patch of skin that a dog makes by licking, chewing or scratching one spot. It can appear suddenly, grow fast and hide under thick fur. Dogs with a thick or long coat get them more often. The usual places are the cheek, neck, outer thigh and base of the tail.

When to See the Vet

Book a visit as soon as you notice a hotspot: it hurts and can quickly get worse. We can usually see your dog the same or next working day. Call us straight away if it keeps growing or has bloody crusts, or if the skin around it is swollen, thick or dotted with small bumps (signs of a deeper infection). Also call straight away if your dog seems unwell or will not let you touch it, or if sores appear on the back within a few days of a bath or grooming. If any of this happens outside opening hours, call the regional emergency vet on 0600 399 299.

First Aid at Home

Stop your dog from licking or scratching the spot. A protective cone works best (we sell them), and a clean T-shirt or body suit can cover a sore on the body. Be gentle: a dog in pain may snap. Keep the spot clean and dry, and put nothing on it unless we advise it: dogs lick it off, and some essential oils are poisonous. Don’t cut the hair with scissors: it is easy to cut the skin.

At the Clinic

We clip the hair around the sore and clean it, often under light sedation because it hurts. Home treatment usually means a cleaning product made for dogs, a medicine to put on the skin and something for itch and pain. An ordinary hotspot does not need antibiotic tablets; they are for deeper infections. With treatment, most heal within two weeks. We also look for the cause, for example by checking a skin or ear sample under the microscope during the visit. Book online (Akuutti ja sairas lemmikki → Sairaskäynti, muu vaiva) or call us.

Why Hotspots Happen

A hotspot starts when something makes the dog itch or hurt and it keeps licking or scratching the same place. Triggers include allergies, fleas, ear infections, anal gland problems, insect bites and pain, such as from sore joints. A wet or matted coat may add to the risk.

Preventing New Hotspots

Brush out dead undercoat regularly, especially when your dog is moulting, and dry the coat well after swimming. Treat ear infections, itching and any fleas early. If hotspots keep coming back, let us look for the cause: it is often an allergy.
