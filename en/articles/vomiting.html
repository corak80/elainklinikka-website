Vomiting in Dogs and Cats — When to Call and What to Do at Home

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

An adult dog or cat that vomits once or twice but is otherwise bright often gets better within a day. Vomiting can also be a sign of poisoning, a swallowed object or serious illness.

Call at Once

Call us on (06) 321 7300 at once if your pet may have eaten something poisonous or swallowed an object such as a toy, sock, bone or string (if you see string under its tongue or at its bottom, don’t pull it); there is blood in the vomit (red, or dark like coffee grounds); your dog retches again and again but brings up nothing or only a little foam, especially if it is restless or drooling or its belly looks swollen; a male cat is straining in the litter tray (its bladder may be blocked); or your pet struggles to breathe, collapses or is very weak. Outside opening hours, call the regional emergency vet on 0600 399 299. Don’t try to make your pet vomit yourself: home methods can be dangerous.

Call the Same Day

Call the same day if your pet vomits several times or cannot keep water down; there is blood in its stool; it is dull, has a painful belly, dry gums or sunken eyes, or has refused food for a day; it is a puppy or kitten; or it is elderly, has a long-term illness or takes medicine from a vet — if it is pain relief, give no more until you have spoken to us.

Vomiting or Bringing Up Food?

Before vomiting, a pet often licks its lips, drools or swallows; then its belly heaves and what comes up is often partly digested food or yellow fluid. Food, froth or saliva that comes up without effort, often undigested, is regurgitation: it has other causes, so tell the vet which one you saw. A dog that vomits yellow foam once, on an empty stomach and often early in the morning, and is then its normal self, may be helped by a small late-evening meal; if it keeps happening, book a visit to rule out other causes.

Home Care for Adults

If your adult pet is otherwise healthy, has vomited once or twice and is bright, give no food for a few hours, but don’t take its water away: offer a little at a time, often. Once it keeps water down, give small, frequent meals of a bland, easily digestible food, nothing fatty. We sell easily digestible diets. Cats must not go without food for long: a few days of eating little or nothing can cause a dangerous liver disease. Don’t give human medicines. If the vomiting starts again, or your pet is not back to normal within a day, call us.

Puppies and Kittens

Puppies and kittens dehydrate quickly, and parvovirus and panleukopenia can be life-threatening, especially if not fully vaccinated. Call the same day if a puppy or kitten vomits.

Cats and Hairballs

An occasional hairball can be normal, but book a visit if your cat vomits more than once a week, or vomits and is losing weight: kidney disease, an overactive thyroid and bowel disease are common causes, especially in older cats. A cat that crouches with its neck stretched out and hacks without bringing anything up may be coughing, for example from asthma: call us the same day, and film it if you can. If it struggles to breathe or breathes with its mouth open, call at once. Regular brushing helps reduce hairballs.

At the Clinic

When needed, we do blood tests (with results during the visit), X-rays or an ultrasound scan. A swallowed object still in the stomach can often be removed with an endoscope, without surgery; if that fails, or the object has moved on into the intestine, surgery is usually needed. If your pet needs a drip, it can stay with us for the day. You can book online (Akuutti ja sairas lemmikki → Äkillisen oksentelun/ripulin ensiapu) or call us.

Contact Us

Unsure? Call us on (06) 321 7300 or email info@saarivet.fi with photos. If it is urgent, call — don’t email.
