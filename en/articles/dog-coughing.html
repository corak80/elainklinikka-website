Dog Coughing – Causes, When to See a Vet and Treatment

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

An occasional cough, for example after drinking too fast, is usually harmless. A cough that keeps coming back, gets worse or comes with other signs is a symptom: the cause can be an infection, the heart, the windpipe, the lungs or, less often in Finland, lungworms.

When is a cough an emergency?

Call at once if your dog struggles to breathe at rest, cannot lie down, has pale, grey or bluish gums, collapses, coughs up blood or seems to be choking. Breathing difficulty can mean fluid in the lungs from heart failure, even without a cough. Also call at once if a large dog keeps retching without bringing anything up, which can be a twisted stomach, or if your dog may have eaten rat or mouse bait: some baits stop the blood from clotting, and coughing blood usually starts three to seven days later. Call us on (06) 321 7300; out of hours, call the regional out-of-hours emergency number, 0600 399 299.

Cough, gagging or reverse sneezing?

A cough often sounds like “something stuck in the throat”: the dog stretches its neck, coughs hard and ends by gagging or bringing up white foam, which is usually mucus and saliva, not stomach contents. In reverse sneezing the dog stands still and pulls air in through its nose in loud snorts for a few seconds to a minute or two. It is common in small and flat-faced dogs and usually harmless.

Kennel cough

Kennel cough is a contagious airway infection that spreads where dogs meet; most dogs recover in one to three weeks. Call before you come in, and keep your dogs away from other dogs until the cough has been gone for at least two weeks. Contact us the same day if your dog is tired, will not eat, has a fever or breathes fast, or if it is a puppy, old or ill, because it can develop pneumonia. Read about the vaccines on our vaccinations page.

Heart disease and coughing

In older small-breed dogs, a heart murmur is often caused by a leaking mitral valve. A very enlarged heart can cause a cough even without heart failure, so a cough alone does not show whether a dog has heart failure. The warning sign is fast breathing at rest and in sleep. Count your dog’s breaths for one minute while it sleeps: under 30 is normal. If it is above 30 on repeated counts, contact us the same day; if your dog also struggles to breathe, call at once. If a dog with a known heart murmur starts coughing, contact us the same day.

Windpipe, bronchitis and other causes

In small breeds such as the Yorkshire Terrier, Pomeranian and Chihuahua, the windpipe can weaken and flatten from middle age on, causing a harsh, honking cough with excitement or pulling on the collar. Most dogs improve with medicines, weight loss and a harness, usually for life; if a stent is needed, we refer. A cough on most days for at least two months may be chronic bronchitis. In older large dogs, especially Labradors, laryngeal paralysis causes noisy breathing, a changed bark and poor tolerance of heat; if breathing gets difficult, call at once.

Lungworms

Dogs get lungworms by eating snails, slugs or frogs. In Finland the fox lungworm is common in foxes but rarely found in pet dogs, and the French heartworm has so far been reported in only a few dogs. Lungworms can cause a cough that does not go away, and the French heartworm can also cause bleeding. We look for larvae in faecal samples sent to a laboratory; several samples may be needed, because one negative result does not rule lungworm out. Not every dewormer works against lungworms, so ask us which one your dog needs.

Examination and treatment

Tell us if your dog has been abroad or was imported: heartworm, a different worm spread by mosquitoes mainly in southern and south-eastern Europe, also causes coughing. Depending on the findings, we use chest X-rays, a heart ultrasound and blood tests, including the NT-proBNP heart test, and if needed examine the airways with a bronchoscope under general anaesthesia. Treatment depends on the cause. Never give your dog a cough or cold medicine meant for people: they can contain painkillers, decongestants or xylitol that are poisonous to dogs, and they can hide a serious disease.

When to book a visit

Book a visit if the cough gets worse, lasts more than ten days, keeps coming back or comes at night. Until then, use a harness instead of a collar, avoid tobacco smoke and film a coughing episode on your phone. Call us on (06) 321 7300 or book online.
