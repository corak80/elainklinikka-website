Fever in Dogs and Cats — Normal Temperature and How to Measure It

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

A healthy dog’s or cat’s temperature is about 38–39 °C. Above about 39.5 °C is a fever. A warm or dry nose doesn’t show a fever: only a thermometer does. Puppies and kittens under four weeks run cooler.

How to Measure

Use an ordinary digital thermometer in your pet’s bottom; ear and forehead thermometers are less reliable, even those sold for pets. Put a little petroleum jelly or oil on the tip, lift the tail, slide the thermometer in gently about 2 cm (1.5 cm in a cat), slightly to the side, and hold it until it beeps. Exercise and excitement can raise the reading: if it is high but under 40 °C and your pet seems well, let it rest and measure again. If your pet resists, don’t force it: call us.

Signs of Fever

A pet with a fever is often dull, tired, off its food, hiding or shivering, and may breathe faster. Common causes are infections, such as a bite abscess in a cat, and inflammation.

Call at Once

Call us on (06) 321 7300 at once if the temperature is 40 °C or more, or under 37.5 °C in a pet that seems unwell, or if your pet is very weak, collapses, struggles to breathe, has pale gums or has a seizure. A dog that is hot and panting heavily after exercise, on a warm day or after being left in a car may have heatstroke: move it somewhere cool, pour cold tap water over its body but not its face, and let a fan or breeze blow over it; don’t cover it with a wet towel. Then call us and keep cooling it on the way. Outside opening hours, call the regional emergency vet on 0600 399 299.

Call the Same Day

Call us the same day if your pet has a fever and is dull or off its food, if the fever lasts more than a day or starts after an operation, or if a puppy or kitten has a fever. After a vaccination, a slightly raised temperature, tiredness or a poorer appetite can occur and usually pass within a day or two: call us if they last longer. Don’t give your pet fever or pain medicines meant for people: some are poisonous to pets even in small amounts.

At the Clinic

A fever is a sign, not a disease, so we look for the cause, when needed with blood tests (results during the visit), an X-ray or ultrasound. We treat the cause and give a drip in day care if your pet is dehydrated. Book online (Akuutti ja sairas lemmikki → Sairaskäynti, muu vaiva) or call us.
