Dog or Cat Scared of Fireworks? How to Get Through New Year

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

Many dogs and cats fear fireworks: they may tremble, pant, hide, bark, try to escape or refuse food. The fear seldom passes by itself and can worsen with time, so plan ahead. In Finland, fireworks are allowed on New Year’s Eve from 6 pm to 2 am, except where the rescue services have banned them, such as Vaasa city centre. Bangs are often heard for days before and after.

Start weeks before New Year

If your pet was afraid last year, ask your vet early about medicines: some must be started days ahead, and a trial run on a quiet day is wise. Never give your pet medicine a vet has not prescribed for it. Set up a safe den: a quiet corner, open crate or room your pet already likes. Whenever a puppy, or a dog not yet afraid, hears a bang, give it a treat straight away, so bangs come to mean treats.

On New Year’s Eve

Walk your dog in daylight, and keep it on a lead outdoors, even in your garden. Keep your cat indoors from before dark, with its hiding places, litter tray and water. Close windows and curtains, and turn on the TV or music. Let your pet hide where it wants; don’t pull it out. Calmly comfort your pet if it comes to you, and if it will eat, give a small treat after each bang. Never punish a scared pet. Stay at home with your pet if you can.

If your pet runs away

A scared pet may bolt through an open door or slip its collar, so check that your dog’s collar or harness fits snugly and has a tag with your phone number. Your dog must be microchipped and in Ruokavirasto’s dog register: log in at koirarekisteri.ruokavirasto.fi and check that your phone number there is current. Microchip your cat too, though it is not yet compulsory. If your pet goes missing, call the lost-animal shelter and give the chip number: for Vaasa, Mustasaari, Vöyri and Maalahti, that is Koiratarha Kulkuri, 040 834 2222.

When to see the vet

Contact your vet if your pet panics, hurts itself or destroys things, or if its fear is worsening. A sudden new noise fear in an adult pet, especially an older dog, can signal pain or illness. After New Year, keep your dog away from firework remains: they can be poisonous, so call a vet if it eats any (see our poisoning article). When the bangs are over, ask your vet about training with firework recordings, played very quietly at first and paired with treats: it takes weeks or months. Call us on (06) 321 7300. If your pet is hurt or poisoned out of hours, call the regional emergency vet on 0600 399 299.
