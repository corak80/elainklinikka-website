Dog and Cat Deworming — How Often for Puppies, Kittens and Adults

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

How often a pet needs deworming depends on its risk. A healthy, low-risk adult can have a faecal sample checked instead and be treated only when needed. The advice below follows the European ESCCAP guideline and the Finnish Food Authority (Ruokavirasto), checked in November 2026.

How Often Should a Dog Be Dewormed?

– Almost always indoors, no contact with other dogs, no parks or gardens, no raw food: 1–2 times a year, or a faecal sample as often.
– Family dog that meets other dogs on walks and in parks: about 4 times a year, or a faecal sample as often.
– Hunting dogs and dogs that eat prey or carcasses, or raw meat, fish or offal that has not first been frozen for a week: a tapeworm treatment every month, plus roundworm treatment or a faecal sample 4–12 times a year. The lungs and other offal of moose and reindeer should always be cooked thoroughly before a dog eats them.
– Monthly deworming or a monthly faecal sample may be advised in homes with small children, elderly people or anyone with a weakened immune system.

How Often Should a Cat Be Dewormed?

– Indoor cat that does not hunt or eat raw meat: 1–2 times a year, or a faecal sample as often.
– Outdoor cat: 4–12 times a year — about 4 times if it rarely hunts, up to monthly if it hunts mice and other rodents.
– Cat fed raw meat, fish or offal that has not first been frozen for a week: at least 4 times a year.
– Monthly deworming or a monthly faecal sample may be advised in homes with small children, elderly people or anyone with a weakened immune system.

Puppies and Kittens

Puppies are dewormed for the first time at 2 weeks and kittens at 3 weeks of age, because roundworms pass to them from the mother. Treatment is repeated every 2 weeks until 2 weeks after weaning, then monthly until 6 months of age for puppies and kittens that remain at risk; the others move on to the adult schedule. The mother is treated at the same time as the puppies’ or kittens’ first treatment. Ask the breeder for the deworming record, and we will help you continue.

When Is a Faecal Sample Enough?

A faecal sample can replace deworming if it is taken as often as the deworming would be. Faecal tests miss many tapeworms, so animals that hunt or eat raw meat still need regular tapeworm treatment. Collect fresh faeces on three consecutive days into one container and bring it to the clinic.

Signs of Worms

Many infected adults show no signs. Watch for a swollen belly in puppies and kittens, weight loss, diarrhoea or vomiting, dragging the bottom, and rice-grain-like segments around the anus. Roundworms can also infect people, especially small children, so pick up faeces promptly and wash your hands.

Which Dewormer to Choose?

The right product depends on the parasite and the animal’s weight. Several dewormers are sold at pharmacies without a prescription; others need a veterinary prescription — ask us which one fits your pet.

Tapeworm Treatment for Dogs That Travel

By law, a dog coming to Finland from abroad, including from Sweden and on the Vaasa–Umeå ferry, must be treated against the fox tapeworm by a vet abroad 1–5 days before arrival. For a trip of under 24 hours, or if the dog never leaves the car or ship, use the 28-day rule instead; it also suits frequent trips. No treatment is needed for a dog coming directly from Norway, Ireland or Malta, but a dog returning from Norway via Sweden needs it. Cats do not need the treatment.
