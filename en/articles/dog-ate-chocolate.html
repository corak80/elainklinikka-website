Dog Ate Chocolate – How Much Is Dangerous and What to Do

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

Chocolate is one of the most common causes of poisoning in dogs, especially at Christmas and Easter. Whether it is dangerous depends on the type of chocolate, how much your dog ate and what it weighs. If you are not sure, call your vet: chocolate poisoning is easiest to treat before any signs appear.

Why is chocolate poisonous to dogs?

Chocolate contains theobromine and caffeine. Dogs break down theobromine much more slowly than people do, so it stays in the body for a long time and, together with caffeine, overstimulates the heart and nervous system. The darker the chocolate, the more of these substances it contains: cocoa powder has the most, dark chocolate several times more than milk chocolate, and white chocolate almost none. Chocolate is poisonous to cats too, but they rarely eat it.

How much chocolate is dangerous?

As a rough guide, mild signs can start from these amounts: for a 5 kg dog, about 9 g of 70% dark chocolate or 40 g of milk chocolate; for a 10 kg dog, 18 g or 85 g; for a 20 kg dog, 35 g or 170 g; and for a 30 kg dog, 55 g or 260 g. Twice these amounts can cause heart problems, and three times can cause seizures: a 100 g bar of 70% dark chocolate can make even a 40 kg dog ill and can cause heart problems in a 20 kg dog. Pure cocoa powder is stronger still: a tablespoon of it can make a small dog ill. Chocolate cake, biscuits and filled chocolates can be dangerous too, and may also contain raisins, macadamia nuts or xylitol. Online calculators give only an estimate, so call your vet even for a smaller amount.

Signs of chocolate poisoning

Signs usually start within 6–12 hours. Early signs are vomiting, diarrhoea, thirst, restlessness and panting. Larger amounts can cause a racing heart, trembling, a high body temperature and seizures. In severe cases the signs can last up to three days. A dog that has vomited can still be in danger, because some of the chocolate may already have been absorbed.

What to do if your dog ate chocolate

Call your vet straight away. Keep the wrapper and say what kind of chocolate it was, roughly how much, when, and what your dog weighs. Do not try to make your dog vomit yourself: salt and hydrogen peroxide can do serious harm. If your dog comes in early, the vet can often make it vomit safely before much of the poison is absorbed. Outside opening hours, call the regional out-of-hours emergency number, 0600 399 299.

Treatment at the vet

If it is not too late, the vet makes your dog vomit and may give something that binds the poison in the gut. A dog with signs may need a drip and care to calm its heart and nervous system, and some need to stay in hospital until the signs settle. With early treatment, most dogs recover fully.
