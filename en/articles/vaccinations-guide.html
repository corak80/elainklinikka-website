Dog and Cat Vaccination Schedule — Puppies, Kittens and Adults

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

Vaccinations protect pets from serious infectious diseases. Vaccination protocols vary and change over time. We follow the current recommendations of the Finnish Food Authority (Ruokavirasto) and each vaccine’s instructions; where they give no specific advice, we use the WSAVA guidelines (2024) and, for cats, the Catus recommendations (2025). The vet always assesses each pet individually. Recommendations checked in November 2026.

Vaccination Schedule: Puppies, Kittens and Adults

Puppies (DHPPi):
– First vaccination usually at 12 weeks (at 6–8 weeks if the risk of infection is higher), then every 3–4 weeks until about 16 weeks of age.
– At 16 weeks: DHPPi and rabies.
– One year after the last puppy vaccination: booster (DHPPi and rabies).
– Adult dogs: DHPPi and rabies every 3 years. Kennel cough vaccine every year for dogs that stay in kennels or often go to shows and other dog events.

Kittens (RCP):
– First vaccination usually at 12 weeks (at 6–8 weeks if the risk of infection is higher), then every 3–4 weeks until about 16 weeks of age.
– At 16 weeks: RCP and rabies (recommended especially for cats that go outside).
– One year after the last kitten vaccination: booster (RCP, and rabies if it was given).
– Adult cats: RCP and rabies every 3 years. Cats in contact with other cats may need the cat flu components every year; we assess this at the health check.

The series continues until about 16 weeks because antibodies from the mother can block the vaccine until then. Deworm puppies and kittens at least 10 days before vaccination, and tell us if your pet is pregnant or is taking cortisone. Until the puppy series is complete, let your puppy meet only healthy, fully vaccinated dogs you know and avoid dog parks; puppy classes are fine once at least a week has passed since the first vaccination.

What If a Vaccination Is Late?

A late booster rarely means starting over. If the earlier vaccinations were complete, one dose is usually enough. If several years have passed — for cats, more than 3 — or the history is unknown, the vet may give two doses 3–4 weeks apart. If 18 months or more have passed since the last leptospirosis vaccination, the course is restarted with two doses. A rabies vaccination given after a lapse counts as a first vaccination and is valid for travel only 21 days later.

Vaccines for Dogs

The DHPPi vaccine protects against distemper, infectious hepatitis, parvovirus and parainfluenza, one of the causes of kennel cough. Ruokavirasto recommends rabies vaccination for all pet dogs; it is compulsory for hunting dogs and official service dogs. The kennel cough vaccine (parainfluenza + Bordetella) is recommended for dogs in kennels or at shows, and many events require it. Leptospirosis vaccination is recommended for dogs travelling to southern Europe, Asia or North Africa, and a few cases are found in Finland each year. It needs two doses 2–4 weeks apart and then a booster every year, so start at least two months before a trip.

Vaccines for Cats

The RCP vaccine against panleukopenia, calicivirus and herpesvirus is recommended for all cats, including indoor cats. The cat flu components make the illness milder but do not prevent infection. Rabies vaccination is not compulsory, but Ruokavirasto recommends it for all pet cats and it is required for travel; we recommend it especially for cats that go outside, mainly because of bats. Feline leukaemia (FeLV) vaccination is considered case by case, as FeLV is almost non-existent in Finland.

Side Effects

Side effects are uncommon and usually pass within a day or two: a brief fever, tiredness, reduced appetite or a sore, swollen injection site. Contact us if they last longer than two days. Serious allergic reactions are rare and usually start within 15 minutes: facial swelling, hives, vomiting, difficulty breathing or collapse — contact a vet immediately. Waiting 15–30 minutes at the clinic after a vaccination is always a good idea, and we strongly recommend it after the first vaccinations. In cats, have a vet check any injection-site lump that is still there after 3 months, is larger than 2 cm, or is still growing a month after the injection.

Rabies in Finland

Finland has been rabies-free since 1991, but rabies-related viruses have been found in bats (2009, 2016 and 2017). The Finnish Kennel Club requires valid rabies, distemper, parvovirus and hepatitis vaccinations for every dog at shows, trials and other dog events. If a vaccination has lapsed, the dog can attend only after the vaccine’s waiting period (usually 21–30 days).

Travelling with Your Pet

Within the EU, Norway and Switzerland, your pet needs a microchip, a valid rabies vaccination and an EU pet passport. The first rabies vaccination becomes valid no earlier than 21 days after it is given. Dogs returning to Finland, including from Sweden, need tapeworm treatment by a vet 1–5 days before arrival (not when coming directly from Norway, Ireland or Malta); for frequent trips, the 28-day rule can be used instead. Travel outside the EU may also require a rabies antibody test months in advance. Read more on our EU pet passport page, and check the current rules with Ruokavirasto and the destination country before travelling. Rules checked in November 2026.
