Urinary Tract Infection in Dogs and Cats — Signs, Tests and When to Call

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

A urinary tract infection is a bacterial infection, usually of the bladder. It is common in dogs, especially females, but uncommon in cats under about ten: their bladder trouble is usually an inflammation without bacteria (often linked to stress), stones or, in males, a urethral plug. The signs are the same, so only tests show which it is.

Signs

Peeing often and only a little, straining, blood in the urine, licking the genitals, or accidents indoors in a house-trained dog. A cat may pee outside the litter tray.

Call at Once

Call us on (06) 321 7300 at once if your pet strains but passes no urine or only drops: the urethra may be blocked, which can kill within a day or two. A male cat that strains or keeps going to the litter tray is an emergency, even if it looks like constipation. Also call at once if a pet with urinary signs vomits, stops eating, is very dull or has a painful belly. Outside opening hours, call the regional emergency vet on 0600 399 299.

Call the Same Day

Call us the same day about any other urinary signs. Don’t give leftover antibiotics: they may be the wrong ones and spoil the urine culture. Home remedies such as cranberry are unproven.

Common Causes

In dogs, bacteria from around the genitals and anus travel up into the bladder; stones, diabetes and other diseases make this more likely, and repeat infections need a search for the cause. In cats, stress, too little water and an overweight, indoor life raise the risk of bladder inflammation; less stress helps prevent new bouts, and more water may too. Cats over about ten, especially those with kidney disease, diabetes or an overactive thyroid, get infections more often.

At the Clinic

You don’t need to bring a urine sample. We take it straight from the bladder with a fine needle guided by ultrasound, usually without sedation; the ultrasound also checks for bladder stones. A culture shows whether there are bacteria and which antibiotic works, usually by the next day. We don’t give antibiotics blindly: unnecessary antibiotics breed resistant bacteria. Some stones can be dissolved with a special diet; others are removed if they cause trouble. We sell urinary diets for cats and dogs; the right one depends on the stone.
