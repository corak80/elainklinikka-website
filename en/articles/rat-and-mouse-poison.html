Rat and Mouse Poison – Dangers to Dogs and Cats, and What to Do

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

Rat and mouse poisons are a common cause of poisoning in dogs, and cats can be poisoned by eating a poisoned mouse. The mouse poisons sold to consumers in Finland are currently all blood-thinning poisons, which cause signs only days later. If your pet has eaten poison or a poisoned rodent, or might have, call your vet straight away, even if it seems well.

Can a cat be poisoned by eating a poisoned mouse?

Yes. Alpha-chloralose, sold to consumers until 2023, has poisoned many cats in Finland, and one poisoned mouse can be enough. Mice that have eaten a blood-thinning poison live on for days, so a cat that eats several can be poisoned too.

Signs

Blood-thinning poisons cause bleeding, usually three to seven days later: the pet becomes tired and weak, with pale gums, laboured breathing, nosebleeds, black or bloody stools, or bruises. Bleeding inside the body can be life-threatening even when no blood is visible. Alpha-chloralose acts within hours: the pet trembles, is wobbly, sleepy or cold, or has seizures.

What to do

Call your vet straight away, even if it happened a day or two ago: with blood-thinning poisons there is often time to treat before bleeding starts. Say what your pet ate, how much, when, and roughly what it weighs. Take the packaging, or a photo of it, with you. Do not try to make your pet vomit yourself. Wrap a pet that is trembling, wobbly or cold in a blanket. Outside opening hours, call the regional out-of-hours emergency number, 0600 399 299.

Treatment at the vet

If your pet ate the poison within the last few hours, the vet can often make it vomit safely. With a blood-thinning poison, the vet either checks blood clotting after about two days or prescribes an antidote for several weeks; finish the whole course. There is no antidote to alpha-chloralose, but with warmth and care most cats recover within a few days.

Prevention

In Finland, consumers may use mouse poison only indoors, ready-filled in bait boxes. The Finnish Safety and Chemicals Agency (Tukes) advises using snap traps before poison, and avoiding it in homes with pets. If you use it, place the boxes out of your pets’ reach, keep pets from roaming freely, and pick up dead mice with gloves. Read about other poisons in our poisoning article.
