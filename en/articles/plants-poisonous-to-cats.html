Plants Poisonous to Cats – Lilies, Christmas Flowers and Houseplants

Veterinarian Assaf Wydra, Eläinklinikka Saari

Many cats like to nibble plants, and many common flowers and houseplants are poisonous to them. Most cause only a sore mouth, drooling or vomiting, but lilies can kill a cat. If your cat has chewed a plant and you are not sure it is safe, call your vet and take the plant, or a photo of it, with you.

Lilies are the most dangerous

True lilies (Lilium) and day lilies (Hemerocallis) cause sudden kidney failure in cats. Every part is poisonous — the flowers, leaves, stems and pollen — and so is the vase water. A cat can be poisoned by licking pollen off its fur or by nibbling a single leaf. Vomiting, drooling and tiredness come first, within hours, and kidney failure follows within one to three days. A cat that gets to the vet quickly, before the kidneys are damaged, usually recovers; if treatment starts only once the kidneys are failing, the outlook is poor. Do not keep lilies in a home or garden with a cat; “pollen-free” lilies are not safe either. Peace lilies and calla lilies are not true lilies and usually only irritate the mouth, but lily of the valley, Finland’s national flower, contains heart poisons and is dangerous too.

Christmas flowers

The poinsettia is less dangerous than its reputation suggests: its sap usually only irritates the mouth and stomach. Amaryllis and hyacinth are more harmful, especially their bulbs, and can cause vomiting, diarrhoea and drooling. Christmas roses can cause drooling, stomach pain and diarrhoea, and azaleas can also affect the heart. Lilies in Christmas bouquets are the biggest danger.

Common houseplants

Many popular houseplants, such as monstera, dieffenbachia, philodendron and pothos, contain tiny crystals that cause mouth pain, drooling, pawing at the mouth and vomiting. This is unpleasant but rarely dangerous. Aloe vera and kalanchoe can cause vomiting and diarrhoea, and kalanchoe can, rarely, affect the heart. The sago palm is very poisonous and can cause liver failure. In spring, tulips and daffodils are poisonous too, especially their bulbs. Outdoors, lily of the valley, yew, foxglove and rhododendron are among the most dangerous plants.

Safer plants for a home with a cat

Spider plants, Christmas cacti, moth orchids and African violets are listed as non-toxic to cats, and a pot of cat grass gives a cat something to nibble instead of your houseplants. Any plant, even grass, can still cause vomiting. Before buying a new plant, check it against a list of plants poisonous to pets, such as the ASPCA’s.

If your cat has chewed a plant

Call your vet. Say what the plant is, if you know, which part your cat ate and when, and take the plant, or a photo of it, with you. Do not try to make your cat vomit yourself. With lilies, do not wait for signs: wipe any pollen off your cat’s fur with a damp cloth so that it cannot lick off any more, and go to a vet straight away. Outside opening hours, call the regional out-of-hours emergency number, 0600 399 299. Read more about other poisons in our poisoning article.
