## Part 3 — Hostile read of the whole current master: remaining issues

Severity key: R = should be fixed before publishing; M = minor, optional polish.

### R1 — Home subcutaneous fluids presented without the safety frame (owner safety)
- Current sentence (treatment.text): "plenty of water and — when needed — fluid therapy given under the skin at home or intravenously at the clinic,"
- Problem: ISFM 2016 (p.228): SC fluids "75–150 ml every 1–3 days … most commonly employed in cats with advanced (stages 3 and 4) CKD, but should be considered on a case-by-case basis. Cats should be carefully monitored to ensure there is clinical benefit and to avoid overhydration." IRIS cat 2026 likewise: "some of these cats may require maintenance fluids administered routinely". Owners reading "at home" with no qualifier may self-start or over-dose; over-hydration is dangerous in the cardiac/hypertensive CKD cat. The first report flagged this as safety item 2; it was not addressed.
- Source: ISFM 2016 consensus, JFMS 18:219, section "Hydration"; IRIS Cat 2026 stage 3–4 hydration notes.
- Replacement: "plenty of water and — when needed — fluid therapy, either intravenously at the clinic or, for selected patients, under the skin at home after we have taught you the technique and with regular checks, because too much fluid can be harmful (especially in cats with heart disease),"

### R2 — Acute kidney injury recovery overstated
- Current sentence (causes.text): "Acute failure is an emergency, but if treated in time the kidneys can recover; chronic failure cannot be reversed, only slowed."
- Problem: implies recovery is the rule with timely treatment. Merck: "Severe AKI that necessitates medical intervention is a serious condition, with a survival rate of approximately 50%." Ethylene-glycol and lily cases that present late do not recover even with treatment. First report marked this VERIFIED "with caveat"; the caveat was not added.
- Source: Merck Veterinary Manual, Renal dysfunction in dogs and cats (AKI section).
- Replacement: "Acute failure is an emergency: treated quickly, the kidneys can often recover, but not always — roughly half of severe cases are lost — whereas chronic failure cannot be reversed, only slowed."

### R3 — Prognosis paragraph: stage-2 figure and "treated animals outlive them" double-count the treatment effect and mismatch the article's own stage-2 definition
- Current sentences (prognosis.text): "in the largest study, cats diagnosed in IRIS stage 2 lived a median of about 3 years, in stage 3 about 2 years, and in stage 4 about 3 months." … "with a renal diet, treatment and regular monitoring, individual animals frequently outlive them."
- Problem (internal contradiction + overstatement): (a) The stages section defines stage 2 as "creatinine normal or slightly raised", but Boyd 2008 enrolled only cats with creatinine >2.3 mg/dl — its "stage 2" is late stage 2 (IIb, 2.3–2.8 mg/dl). Cats caught early in stage 2 (creatinine 1.6–2.3, or SDMA-only) are not represented; their expected survival is longer. The number is therefore conservative but is being attached to a broader group than it was measured in. (b) Boyd's cats were referral patients that were, in general, already receiving renal diets and treatment; the closing clause implies the medians are for untreated animals and that treatment lets pets "outlive" them, which double-counts the diet effect that the diet section already claims.
- Source: Boyd 2008 abstract ("Retrospective case review of 733 cats with SCr > 2.3 mg/dL … 211 cats"; stage IIb 1,151 d); IRIS 2026 staging table (cat stage 2 = 1.6–2.8 mg/dl).
- Replacement (two sentences): "in the largest study with survival by stage, cats diagnosed at the upper end of stage 2 lived a median of about 3 years, in stage 3 about 2 years, and in stage 4 about 3 months — cats caught earlier in stage 2 can be expected to do better." and "These are median values from published studies of treated animals — half of the animals lived longer than these figures — not predictions for your pet: with a well-accepted renal diet, good appetite and regular monitoring, individual animals frequently outlive them."

### M1 — Prevalence hedge
- Current: "30–40 % of cats over 10 years old have some degree of kidney disease, most of it still mild."
- Problem: ISFM writes "may affect ⩾30–40%"; the master drops both the "may" and the "≥". Marino 2014 found 50 % in randomly selected cats (all ages), so the true figure is if anything higher.
- Source: ISFM 2016 p.219; Marino 2014 JFMS 16:465.
- Replacement: "an estimated 30–40 % or more of cats over 10 years old have some degree of kidney disease, most of it still mild."

### M2 — Stage 1 "dilute urine" applies to cats only
- Current: "Stage 1: creatinine normal, but some other kidney abnormality is present — a persistently raised SDMA, dilute urine, protein in the urine, or changes on ultrasound."
- Problem: IRIS 2026: "inadequate urinary concentrating ability without identifiable non-renal cause (in cats not dogs)". Dogs dilute urine for many non-renal reasons.
- Source: IRIS Staging of CKD (modified 2026), Stage 1 comments.
- Replacement: "…a persistently raised SDMA, dilute urine (in cats), protein in the urine, or changes on ultrasound."

### M3 — "slowed a great deal" / "single most effective treatment" / "proven to extend survival" — three intensifiers that outrun the evidence
- Current: (treatment.text) "its progression can be slowed a great deal"; (diet.text) "A therapeutic renal diet is the single most effective treatment"; (treatment.text) "(proven to extend survival)".
- Problem: Ross 2006 (the only feline RCT) found no significant difference in creatinine between groups — it reduced uraemic crises and renal deaths, not measured progression; Jacob 2002 did show slower decline in dogs. ISFM grades renal-diet longevity evidence "GOOD" and calls diet "a mainstay therapy", not "the single most effective". None of these is wrong for an owner article, but together they read as marketing. The first report suggested "can often be slowed considerably", "the treatment with the best evidence", "shown in clinical studies to extend survival".
- Source: Ross 2006 abstract; Jacob 2002 abstract; ISFM 2016 Table 3 / text.
- Replacement: "its progression can often be slowed considerably" ; "A therapeutic renal diet is the treatment with the best evidence behind it" ; "(shown in clinical studies to extend survival)".

### M4 — ISFM's medication rule missing from the diet paragraph
- Current: "…and never while the pet is ill, nauseous or hospitalised, because a food eaten during nausea is often refused for good afterwards."
- Problem: ISFM panel recommendation: "To avoid food aversion, medications should be administered via an alternative, palatable food" — a practical owner instruction the article's own logic (aversion) calls for. Omission, not error.
- Source: ISFM 2016, "Panel recommendations: transitioning cats to a renal diet".
- Replacement (append): "…refused for good afterwards. For the same reason, never hide tablets in the renal food — use a separate treat."

### M5 — RAAS drugs "once stable"
- Current: "blood-pressure medication (amlodipine for cats, ACE inhibitors or telmisartan when protein leaks into the urine)"
- Problem: IRIS cat 2026: "The use of a RAAS inhibitor is contraindicated in any cat that is clinically dehydrated or is showing signs of hypovolemia. Correct dehydration before using these drugs otherwise glomerular filtration rate may drop precipitously." IRIS dog 2026 says the same for ACEI/CCB/ARB. Relevant to owners only in that these drugs are started after stabilisation, not at the first sick visit. Optional.
- Source: IRIS Cat 2026 stage 2 proteinuria notes; IRIS Dog 2026 hypertension notes.
- Replacement: "blood-pressure medication once the patient is stable and hydrated (amlodipine for cats, ACE inhibitors or telmisartan when protein leaks into the urine)"

### M6 — Monitoring paragraph omits stage 1 although the stages section says the stage sets the re-check interval
- Current: "then every 3–6 months in stable stage 2 patients, every 2–3 months in stage 3, and every 1–2 months in stage 4."
- Problem: internal gap only; stage-1 patients exist in the article. ISFM floor "at least every 3–6 months" for all stable cats.
- Source: ISFM 2016 monitoring section; IRIS-derived schedule (stage 1 twice yearly).
- Replacement: "then every 6 months in stage 1, every 3–6 months in stable stage 2 patients, every 2–3 months in stage 3, and every 1–2 months in stage 4."
