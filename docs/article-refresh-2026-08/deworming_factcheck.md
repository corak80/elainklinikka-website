Where Finnish practice deviates from ESCCAP Europe it is noted below; the article should not attribute those deviations to "ESCCAP".

Verdict key: VERIFIED / IMPRECISE / WRONG / UNVERIFIABLE

---

## intro

1. "Modern parasite control is risk-based ... low-risk adult checked with a faecal sample and treated only when needed" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP GL1 Fig. 1/2: faecal examination "at the same frequency as the suggested treatments" is an accepted alternative in every risk group. Source: ESCCAP GL1.

2. "Below are the current recommendations (ESCCAP guidelines)" -> IMPRECISE. Several adult frequencies in the article are Finnish practice, not ESCCAP (see 5, 15). Suggested: "Below are the current recommendations, based on the European ESCCAP guidelines and adapted to the Finnish parasite situation, ..."

## catoften

3. "Adult indoor cat 1–2 times a year, preferably only after a faecal sample" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP Fig. 2 Risk Group A (lives exclusively indoors / fenced patio): "Treat 1–2 times a year against intestinal nematodes or carry out faecal examination and treat according to findings."

4. "A cat that goes outdoors is dewormed about 4 times a year" -> VERIFIED (lower bound). ESCCAP Group B (free to roam, hunting cannot be ruled out): "Treat 4–12 times a year against intestinal nematodes ... Treat 4–12 times a year against tapeworms"; tapeworm section: "tested at least 4 times a year ... or dewormed at least 4 times a year." Suggested small tweak: "at least 4 times a year".

5. "a cat that actively hunts mice ... monthly, because rodents transmit tapeworms" -> VERIFIED with caveat. Monthly (12/yr) is the top of ESCCAP's 4–12 range; ESCCAP gives monthly explicitly only in the E. multilocularis context ("Monthly treatments (12 times a year) prevent egg excretion"), which is not relevant in Finland. Rodent tapeworm = Hydatigera (Taenia) taeniaeformis, ESCCAP: "predominate among tapeworm infections in cats." Suggested: "a cat that actively hunts mice can be treated up to monthly (ESCCAP: 4–12 times a year), because rodents carry the cat tapeworm".

6. "a strictly indoor cat with a clean faecal sample may not need any" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP Group A allows faecal exam instead of treatment.

## kitten

7. "first deworming at 3 weeks of age" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP: "Because prenatal infection does not occur in kittens, fortnightly treatment can begin at three weeks of age". Drontal.fi (Finnish) uses the same 3-week start.

8. "repeated every 2 weeks until 2 weeks after weaning" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP text: "repeated fortnightly until two weeks after weaning" (the Fig. 2 table says "until weaning"; the text version is the stricter one and the article matches it).

9. "then monthly until 6 months of age" -> IMPRECISE. ESCCAP makes this conditional: "If there is an ongoing increased risk of infection (e.g. free roam), monthly treatments should be carried out up to 6 months of age." Suggested: "then, if the kitten has an ongoing infection risk (e.g. outdoor access, several cats), monthly until 6 months of age; otherwise the vet may stop earlier."

10. "The mother is treated at the same time as the litter" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP: "Lactating queens: Deworm at the first treatment of kittens (3 weeks after delivery)."

11. "roundworm larvae pass to them through the milk" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP: "Lactating queens can pass T. cati to their sucking kittens"; Merck: "no prenatal infection occurs" in T. cati. (Merck calls transmammary a minor route overall, but it is the only vertical route in cats, so the sentence stands.)

12. "pot belly, dull coat, diarrhoea and poor weight gain are typical" -> VERIFIED. Merck: "lack of growth and loss of condition ... dull coat and often are 'pot-bellied'."

13. "A kitten you have just adopted should be dewormed on the same schedule if its history is unknown" -> VERIFIED (consistent with ESCCAP young-animal schedule; no explicit ESCCAP sentence).

14. "usually a few days before its first vaccination" -> UNVERIFIABLE / IMPRECISE. Not in ESCCAP GL1 (the word "vaccination" does not occur in GL1). Finnish clinics commonly say 1–2 weeks before vaccination (search summary of Finnish clinic pages: "Pentu madotetaan yleensä 1–2 viikkoa ennen rokotuksia"). Suggested: "in practice the deworming is usually timed 1–2 weeks before the first vaccination".

## indoor

15. "Indoor cats can pick up roundworm eggs carried in on shoes, from flies or from raw meat" -> IMPRECISE. ESCCAP lists eggs "from soil or on fur" and raw meat as Toxocara routes; flies are not mentioned by ESCCAP or Merck. Suggested: "carried in on shoes or on another pet's fur, or from raw meat".

16. "faecal sample once a year and deworming only if parasites are found, or routine treatment 1–2 times a year" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP Group A.

17. "Kittens are always dewormed on the full schedule regardless of lifestyle" -> IMPRECISE (same issue as 9). Suggested: "Kittens are dewormed on the kitten schedule regardless of lifestyle; whether monthly treatment continues to 6 months depends on their risk."

## catsigns

18. "many infected adults show nothing" -> VERIFIED (ESCCAP/Merck; adult infections often subclinical).

19. Sign list (swollen belly, weight loss, diarrhoea/vomiting, dull coat, scooting, rice-grain segments, spaghetti-like worms in vomit/faeces) -> VERIFIED. Merck: "Worms may be vomited and are often voided in the feces". Note "dragging the bottom" is far more often anal-gland related than worms; optional tweak: "occasionally dragging the bottom".

20. "we examine it the same day" -> UNVERIFIABLE (clinic service claim).

## dogoften

21. "An ordinary low-risk family dog is dewormed 1–2 times a year" -> IMPRECISE. ESCCAP Group A (1–2/yr) is defined as a dog that "lives exclusively indoors or has very limited access to the outdoors i.e. it has no direct contact with other dogs and has no access to parks or gardens". A normal Finnish family dog that walks in parks and meets dogs is ESCCAP Group B = "Treat 4 times a year ... or carry out faecal examination". The 1–2/yr figure is Finnish practice (Evidensia: annual faecal test; Drontal.fi: "kaksi kertaa vuodessa"), justified by very low Finnish prevalence (2024 study: 3.5% of 664 dogs; T. canis 0.9%). Suggested: "In Finland, where intestinal worms are rare in pet dogs (about 3–4 % of samples), an ordinary family dog is usually dewormed 1–2 times a year or checked with an annual faecal sample. Note that the European ESCCAP guideline itself puts any dog with park access and dog contact at 4 treatments (or 4 faecal checks) a year."

22. "Higher-risk dogs ... may be treated up to 4 times a year" -> IMPRECISE. ESCCAP Groups C/D (free roaming, hunting, prey/offal, raw meat): "Treat 4–12 times a year against intestinal nematodes" plus "4–12 times a year against tapeworms" (12/yr where Echinococcus risk). Suggested: "at least 4 times a year (ESCCAP: 4–12)".

23. "during the hunting season a monthly tapeworm treatment can be justified" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP: "Dogs that have access to rodents should be treated at four-week intervals with an effective anthelmintic containing praziquantel"; Evidensia flags tapeworm risk in hunting dogs. Also relevant in Finland because E. canadensis occurs in moose/reindeer (see 31).

24. "Dogs on a raw diet fall into the higher-risk group as well" -> IMPRECISE (understated). ESCCAP: "Dogs that are fed raw meat that has not been sufficiently heated (10 minutes, core temperature 65 °C) or frozen (one week, −17 to −20 °C) should be treated for tapeworms every 4 weeks." Suggested: "Dogs on a raw diet are higher-risk too: unless the meat has been frozen for at least a week at −17 to −20 °C (or cooked), ESCCAP recommends a tapeworm treatment every 4 weeks."

## puppy

25. "first time at 2 weeks of age" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP: "Puppies should be treated with appropriate anthelmintics from 14 days old". Evidensia and Drontal.fi: "kahden viikon iässä".

26. "roundworm larvae pass to them already in the womb and through the milk" -> VERIFIED. Merck: "the most common mode of infection is transplacental transfer ... pups may also be infected via the milk."

27. "repeated every 2 weeks until weaning" -> IMPRECISE. ESCCAP: "repeated fortnightly until two weeks after weaning". Suggested: "every 2 weeks until 2 weeks after weaning".

28. "then monthly until 6 months of age" -> IMPRECISE. ESCCAP: "depending on infection risks, monthly treatments carried out up to six months of age" (e.g. puppy playgroups). Evidensia stops the fixed schedule at 10 weeks. Suggested: "and then, depending on the puppy's exposure (puppy classes, dog parks, several dogs at home), monthly until 6 months of age."

29. "the dam is treated together with the litter" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP: "Lactating bitches: Deworm at the first treatment of puppies (2 weeks after delivery)."

30. "continue the monthly schedule at home until the puppy is six months old" -> IMPRECISE (see 28). Suggested: "continue the schedule your vet recommends, typically monthly until six months for puppies with regular dog contact."

## fecal

31. "faecal sample once a year is enough ... if it is clean, no medication is needed" -> VERIFIED for ESCCAP Group A (exam frequency = 1–2/yr). Same caveat as 21: for dogs with park/dog contact ESCCAP wants 4 checks/yr; Finnish practice (Evidensia) accepts annual.

32. "Collect fresh faeces from 2–3 days, mixed in one container" -> IMPRECISE. ESCCAP (GL1 and GL4) recommends faeces "from three consecutive defecations"; pooling is standard for egg flotation (not for lungworm Baermann, where ESCCAP says "samples should not be pooled"). Suggested: "from 3 consecutive days, mixed in one container".

33. "we examine it under the microscope for worm eggs and Giardia" -> UNVERIFIABLE (clinic claim). Giardia needs a specific test (antigen/IFA or centrifugal flotation); if the clinic only does simple flotation, drop "and Giardia".

34. "drug resistance — already a problem in some worms" -> VERIFIED, but geographically misleading. ESCCAP: "virtually no proven cases of anthelmintic resistance to intestinal ... nematodes in dogs and cats in Europe. However, in the USA ... Ancylostoma caninum ... Australia and the USA"; "no evidence of anthelmintic resistance in Toxocara". Suggested: "which is already a problem in some worms abroad (e.g. hookworms in the USA)".

## parasites

35. "Roundworms (Toxocara) are the most common, especially in puppies and kittens" -> IMPRECISE for dogs, VERIFIED for cats. Cats: T. cati is the leading finding (Näreaho 2012: 5.4 %; 2024: 3.3 % pet cats, 34.8 % shelter cats). Dogs: Pullola 2006 T. canis 3.1 % vs Uncinaria 2.6 %; 2024 study strongylid (hookworm-type) eggs 1.7 % vs T. canis 0.9 %, i.e. hookworm eggs were MORE common than Toxocara in Finnish dogs. Suggested: "Roundworms (Toxocara) are the most common worm in cats and in young animals; in adult Finnish dogs the hookworm Uncinaria is found about as often as roundworm."

36. "they are zoonotic — they can infect people, particularly small children" -> VERIFIED. ESCCAP: "Toxocara roundworms have an elevated zoonotic potential ... larva migrans"; Merck: hygiene "particularly in children".

37. "Tapeworms occur mainly in animals that eat rodents or raw meat" -> VERIFIED, incomplete. Add raw fish: Diphyllobothrium latum was found in Finnish dogs (Pullola 0.4 %; 2024 "trematode"/taeniid 0.2 % dogs, 0.6 % cats) and Drontal.fi names raw fish as a risk. Flea tapeworm (Dipylidium) is rare in Finland. Suggested: "...eat rodents, raw meat or raw freshwater fish".

38. "Giardia is a protozoan that causes diarrhoea and spreads through contaminated water and environment" -> VERIFIED (generic). Finnish data: Giardia antigen 3.2 % of cats (Näreaho 2012). Fine as written.

39. "Hookworms and whipworms are rare in Finland" -> WRONG for hookworm, VERIFIED for whipworm. Whipworm: T. vulpis 0.2 % (Pullola 2006 and 2024) = rare. Hookworm: Uncinaria stenocephala 2.6 % (2006) and strongylid eggs 1.7 % (2024), the most or second-most common helminth in Finnish dogs; Pullola: "Kennel housing and visits abroad were identified as risk factors for T. canis and U. stenocephala". Ancylostoma (the tropical hookworm) is what is rare. Suggested: "The northern hookworm (Uncinaria) does occur in Finland, especially in kennel and hunting dogs, and is found about as often as roundworm; whipworm is rare."

40. "The fox tapeworm Echinococcus does not occur in Finland" -> WRONG as worded (IMPRECISE if read narrowly). Correct for E. multilocularis: Ruokavirasto "Myyräekinokokkia ei ole todettu Suomessa eikä myöskään manner-Norjassa"; EU-recognised free status, 300–500 foxes/raccoon dogs tested yearly. But another Echinococcus does occur in Finland: E. canadensis (hirviekinokokki, moose/reindeer–wolf cycle) in North Karelia, Kainuu, Kuusamo, Salla and since 2017 further west; "Myös koira voi saada tartunnan" and Ruokavirasto advises cooking moose/reindeer offal before feeding to dogs. Suggested: "The fox tapeworm Echinococcus multilocularis has never been found in Finland, and the import rules exist to keep it that way. A related species, the moose tapeworm E. canadensis, does occur in eastern and northern Finland in wolves and moose/reindeer, so hunting dogs should never be fed raw moose or reindeer offal."

## product

41. "praziquantel is needed for tapeworms" -> VERIFIED (ESCCAP: "effective anthelmintic containing praziquantel").

42. "roundworms respond to milbemycin, pyrantel, fenbendazole or emodepside" -> VERIFIED (standard, ESCCAP Table of anthelmintics; Merck).

43. "Combination tablets cover both; spot-on products ... for cats; pastes suit puppies and kittens" -> VERIFIED (e.g. Drontal/Milbemax combos; Profender and Dronspot spot-ons; Axilur/Banminth oral suspension/paste). Minor: in Finland the common puppy/kitten form is oral suspension (Axilur vet oraalisuspensio) rather than "paste" — optional tweak: "liquid or paste".

44. "Most dewormers are sold at pharmacies without prescription in Finland" -> IMPRECISE and, given the two actives just named, misleading. OTC (itsehoito) in Finland: fenbendazole (Axilur), flubendazole (Flubenol), pyrantel and pyrantel/praziquantel(/febantel) combos (Drontal, Drontal Comp, Cazitel, Dolpac, Anthelmin, Canex, Dronbits), praziquantel alone (Droncit, Dronspot spot-on) — drontal.fi: "Drontal-tuotteet ilman reseptiä"; apteekkishop.fi lists these. PRESCRIPTION-ONLY (Reseptilääke eläimille, sold only at a pharmacy counter): Milbemax vet (milbemycin/praziquantel), Profender spot-on (emodepside/praziquantel), and every parasite spot-on combo (Advocate, Broadline, Stronghold, NexGard Spectra, Simparica Trio). So the milbemycin and emodepside named in the same paragraph are Rx, and the cat spot-on the paragraph praises (Profender) is Rx while Dronspot is OTC. Suggested: "Many basic dewormers (fenbendazole, pyrantel, praziquantel and their combination tablets such as Drontal, and the praziquantel spot-on Dronspot) are sold at Finnish pharmacies without prescription. Milbemycin (Milbemax), emodepside spot-on (Profender) and the combined flea/tick/worm spot-ons need a prescription from us."

## vaccine

45. "a heavy worm burden can weaken the response to the vaccine" -> UNVERIFIABLE in dogs/cats. Not in ESCCAP GL1 or WSAVA vaccination guidelines; evidence is from mice/humans/cattle and a meta-analysis found deworming before vaccination gave "no overall benefit". Suggested: "a heavy worm burden is thought to be able to blunt the response to a vaccine, and a sick, wormy puppy should not be vaccinated anyway".

46. "the puppy schedule naturally puts a deworming a few days before each vaccination visit" -> IMPRECISE. Finnish practice: 1–2 weeks before vaccination. Suggested: "1–2 weeks before each vaccination visit".

47. "For healthy adults it is not necessary to deworm just because a vaccination is due" -> VERIFIED (no guideline requires it).

## travel

48. "When a dog returns to Finland from abroad, it must by law be treated against the fox tapeworm (Echinococcus) with praziquantel or an equally effective product" -> VERIFIED. Ruokavirasto: "valmisteyhteenvedon mukainen annos pratsikvantelia sisältävää, koiralle hyväksyttyä lääkettä", equally effective substances allowed. Applies to all dogs incl. puppies; not to cats/ferrets ("Kissoja tai frettejä ei tarvitse lääkitä").

49. "given by a vet 1–5 days (24–120 hours) before arrival in Finland and recorded in the pet passport" -> VERIFIED but missing one legally important detail: the dose must be given OUTSIDE Finland ("Lääke pitää antaa Suomen ulkopuolisessa maassa 1–5 vuorokautta (24–120 tuntia) ennen maahantuloa"), and the vet records product, date and time. Suggested: "given by a vet abroad (not in Finland) 1–5 days (24–120 hours) before arrival, and recorded with date and time in the pet passport".

50. "Dogs that travel often can use the 28-day rule: two starting treatments 1–28 days apart" -> VERIFIED. Ruokavirasto FAQ: "kaksi kertaa ennen matkan aloittamista minimissään 1 vuorokauden (24 tuntia) ja maksimissaan 28 vuorokauden välein"; these two may be given in Finland.

51. "then a treatment at intervals of no more than 28 days" -> VERIFIED. FAQ: "kaikissa lääkkeissä antoväli on aina 1–28 vuorokautta", given in any EU country, Norway or Switzerland.

52. "with the last dose given in Finland after the trip" -> VERIFIED. FAQ: "lääkitsee eläinlääkäri Suomessa koiran vielä kerran, jotta 28 vuorokauden lääkityssykli saadaan päätettyä". Missing caveat: the 28-day rule is valid only for travel within the EU, Norway and Switzerland (not third countries), every dose must be given and stamped by a vet. Suggested addition: "(valid for travel within the EU, Norway and Switzerland; every dose must be given and stamped by a vet)".

53. "No treatment is needed for dogs arriving directly from Norway, Ireland or Malta" -> VERIFIED. Ruokavirasto: "Ekinokokkilääkitystä ei vaadita, kun koira tuodaan Suomeen suoraan Norjasta, Irlannista tai Maltalta." Note: the EU Annex (Reg. 2018/878) also lists Northern Ireland as free, but Ruokavirasto's page does not name it; keep the article aligned with Ruokavirasto. Sweden is NOT exempt (FAQ: "Ruotsi ei ole ekinokokkiloisesta vapaa maa") — worth stating explicitly for Vaasa readers who take the ferry to Umeå.

54. "dogs travelling directly from Finland to Norway ... are exempt there" -> VERIFIED. Mattilsynet: "Hunder som kommer direkte til Norge fra Finland, Malta, eller Irland, trenger ikke behandling."

55. "... to Great Britain are exempt there" -> VERIFIED. GOV.UK: "You do not need to treat your dog for tapeworm if you're coming directly to Great Britain from Finland, Ireland, Northern Ireland, Malta or Norway." (Other GB entry requirements — microchip, rabies, passport/AHC, approved route — still apply; a transit through Sweden/Denmark/Germany breaks "directly".)

56. "The full travel requirements are on our EU pet passport page" -> UNVERIFIABLE (internal link).

## cta

57. Clinic service statements -> UNVERIFIABLE, no factual issue.

## Summary of non-VERIFIED items, by severity

1. #40 "Echinococcus does not occur in Finland" — WRONG: E. canadensis (moose tapeworm) occurs in Finnish wolves/moose/reindeer and infects dogs; only E. multilocularis is absent.
2. #44 "Most dewormers are OTC in Finland" — WRONG-ish: Milbemax (milbemycin) and Profender (emodepside), both named in the paragraph, are Reseptilääke; only fenbendazole/flubendazole/pyrantel/praziquantel products are OTC.
3. #39 "Hookworms rare in Finland" — WRONG: Uncinaria is as common as Toxocara in Finnish dogs (2.6 % vs 3.1 % in 2006; strongylid 1.7 % vs T. canis 0.9 % in 2024).
4. #49 Legal rule omits that the 24–120 h dose must be given outside Finland — an owner who doses in Finland before a weekend in Sweden breaks the law.
5. #21/#22/#24 Adult-dog frequencies attributed to ESCCAP are actually Finnish practice: ESCCAP gives 1–2/yr only to indoor-only dogs, 4/yr to any dog with park/dog contact, 4–12/yr to hunters/roamers, and monthly tapeworm treatment for unfrozen raw feeding.
6. #9/#17/#27/#28 Puppy/kitten schedule: "until weaning" should be "until 2 weeks after weaning" (puppies); "monthly until 6 months" is conditional on ongoing risk in ESCCAP, not universal.
7. #35 Toxocara "most common" holds for cats but not for adult Finnish dogs.
8. #45/#46/#14 Vaccine-response claim is unsupported in dogs/cats; timing should be 1–2 weeks, not "a few days".
9. #32 Faecal sample: 3 consecutive days, not 2–3.
10. #34 Resistance: "already a problem" is true in the USA/Australia, "virtually no proven cases" in Europe (ESCCAP).
