## Part 2 — Remaining issues (hostile read of the whole current master)

### MUST FIX

**1. Internal contradiction: faecal-sample section vs dog/cat frequency sections (MEDIUM)**
- Current (fecal.text): "For a healthy adult dog or cat that does not hunt, roam or travel, a faecal sample once a year is enough: if it is clean, no medication is needed."
- Problem: dogoften.text now (correctly) says a typical family dog that meets other dogs in parks needs treatment OR a faecal sample about 4 times a year (ESCCAP Group B), and catoften.text puts an outdoor cat at about 4 times a year. A family dog that walks in parks does not "hunt, roam or travel", so the faecal section tells the same owner once a year is enough. ESCCAP Fig. 1 footnote: "Frequency of alternative testing corresponds to the recommended frequency of treatment."
- Source: ESCCAP GL1 Fig. 1 (Groups A/B + footnote), Fig. 2 (Group A/B).
- Replacement: "For a healthy adult in the low-risk group — an indoor cat, or a dog that is mostly indoors and has little contact with other dogs — a faecal sample once or twice a year is enough: if it is clean, no medication is needed. Dogs and cats with more exposure (park walks and dog contact, outdoor access) are tested at the same frequency at which they would otherwise be treated, about 4 times a year."

**2. 28-day rule missing its legal scope (MEDIUM, legal advice)**
- Current (travel.text): "Dogs that travel often can use the 28-day rule: two starting treatments 1–28 days apart, then a treatment at intervals of no more than 28 days, with the last dose given in Finland after the trip."
- Problem: the rule is only valid for travel within the EU, Norway and Switzerland (a dog on the 28-day cycle returning from e.g. Great Britain or Turkey is non-compliant), the two starting doses are given in Finland before travel, and every dose must be given by a vet and entered in the passport. An owner who self-doses on the cycle breaks the law.
- Source: Ruokavirasto FAQ (re-fetched 29-08-2026): "kaksi kertaa ennen matkan aloittamista minimissään 1 vuorokauden (24 tuntia) ja maksimissään 28 vuorokauden välein"; "Kaksi lääkettä ennen matkustelun alkamista Suomessa, ylläpitävät lääkkeet missä tahansa EU-maassa, Norjassa tai Sveitsissä ja yksi lääke Suomessa kun matkustelu on päättynyt"; "saa se toisesta lääkkeestä lähtien matkustaa vapaasti kaikissa EU-maissa, Norjassa ja Sveitsissä"; "eläinlääkäri merkitsee ne kaikki koiran passiin".
- Replacement: "Dogs that travel often within the EU, Norway and Switzerland can use the 28-day rule: two starting treatments given by a vet in Finland 1–28 days apart, then a treatment at intervals of no more than 28 days in any of those countries, with the last dose given in Finland after the trip — every dose must be given by a vet and entered in the passport."

**3. Resistance overstated for Europe (LOW–MEDIUM, unchanged from report #34)**
- Current (fecal.text): "Treating only when parasites are found avoids needless medication and slows the development of drug resistance — which is already a problem in some worms."
- Problem: implies a current problem locally. ESCCAP: resistance is a USA/Australia phenomenon; virtually none proven in Europe.
- Source: ESCCAP GL1 "Resistance to anthelmintics" (line 1840–1845): "To date, there have been virtually no proven cases of anthelmintic resistance to intestinal and extraintestinal nematodes in dogs and cats in Europe. However, in the USA ... Ancylostoma caninum populations in Australia and the USA."
- Replacement: "Treating only when parasites are found avoids needless medication and slows the development of drug resistance — not yet a proven problem in Europe, but already seen in hookworms in the USA and Australia."

**4. Rx/OTC sentence contradicts itself on praziquantel (LOW)**
- Current (product.text): "In Finland, products containing fenbendazole, flubendazole, pyrantel or praziquantel are sold at pharmacies without prescription, whereas milbemycin and emodepside products need a prescription from the vet."
- Problem: Milbemax (milbemycin + praziquantel), Profender (emodepside + praziquantel), Broadline and NexGard Spectra all "contain praziquantel" and are Rx, so the first clause as written is false and collides with the second. OTC praziquantel is the plain or pyrantel-combination form.
- Source: omaeapteekki.fi Milbemax vet and Profender pages, "Reseptilääke eläimille. ... voidaan ostaa vain apteekin toimipisteestä" (re-fetched); drontal.fi "Apteekista ilman reseptiä"; apteekkishop.fi OTC listing (Axilur, Flubenol, Drontal, Droncit, Dronspot, Cazitel, Dolpac, Anthelmin, Canex).
- Replacement: "In Finland, fenbendazole, flubendazole and pyrantel products, and praziquantel on its own or combined with pyrantel (such as Drontal tablets or the Dronspot spot-on), are sold at pharmacies without prescription, whereas milbemycin (Milbemax), emodepside (Profender) and the combined flea-tick-worm spot-ons need a prescription from the vet."

**5. E. canadensis: reindeer omitted from the dog-infection route (LOW)**
- Current (parasites.text): "...a related moose tapeworm (E. canadensis) is found in wolves, moose and reindeer in eastern and northern Finland and can infect dogs that eat raw moose offal, which is one more reason hunting dogs get regular tapeworm treatment."
- Problem: Ruokavirasto's feeding advice names reindeer as well as moose; in Lapland reindeer offal is the more likely dog food. Otherwise the sentence is correct: E. multilocularis "ei ole todettu Suomessa"; hosts "hirvi, poro tai metsäpeura" / "susi"; "Myös koira voi saada tartunnan"; distribution Pohjois-Karjala, Kainuu, Kuusamo, Salla, and since 2017 also west of that.
- Source: Ruokavirasto Ekinokokit page (re-fetched): "Jos haluaa käyttää poron tai hirven keuhkoja tai muita sisäelimiä koiran rehuna, ne tulee kypsentää huolellisesti"; "koirien säännöllisestä heisimatolääkityksestä ekinokokkiin tehoavalla lääkeaineella, esim. pratsikvantelilla".
- Replacement: "...a related moose tapeworm (E. canadensis) is found in wolves, moose and reindeer in eastern and northern Finland and can infect dogs that eat raw moose or reindeer offal, which is one more reason hunting dogs get regular tapeworm treatment."

**6. Vaccine-response claim still stated as fact (LOW, unchanged from report #45)**
- Current (vaccine.text): "It is good practice, especially for puppies and kittens: a heavy worm burden can weaken the response to the vaccine, and the puppy schedule naturally puts a deworming 1–2 weeks before each vaccination visit."
- Problem: not in ESCCAP GL1 or WSAVA vaccination guidelines for dogs/cats; the Finnish source that states it (drontal.fi, a manufacturer) hedges with "saattavat heikentää" (may weaken).
- Source: ESCCAP GL1 (no occurrence of "vaccinat"); drontal.fi: "Sisäloiset saattavat heikentää rokotteen tehoa, joten on suositeltavaa häätää ne noin kaksi viikkoa ennen rokotuksia."
- Replacement: "It is good practice, especially for puppies and kittens: a heavy worm burden may blunt the response to the vaccine and a wormy puppy is not a healthy puppy to vaccinate, and the puppy schedule naturally puts a deworming 1–2 weeks before each vaccination visit."

### OPTIONAL (unsourced additions or precision tweaks; not counted in the verdict)

**7. "soil in potted plants" — newly introduced, unsourced**
- Current (indoor.text): "Indoor cats can pick up roundworm eggs carried in on shoes or fur, from soil in potted plants or from raw meat"
- Problem: ESCCAP lists "from soil or on fur" and "changing shoes" (line 1963, 2012); potted-plant soil is not in any source and commercial potting soil is not a documented Toxocara reservoir.
- Replacement: "Indoor cats can pick up roundworm eggs carried in on shoes or on another pet's fur, or from raw meat"

**8. Hunting cat "monthly" stated flat**
- Current (catoften.text): "a cat that actively hunts mice and other rodents monthly, because rodents transmit tapeworms."
- Problem: ESCCAP Fig. 2 Group B is 4–12 times a year; "at least 4 times" for hunters, monthly only "to shift the residual risk towards zero" in E. multilocularis areas, which Finland is not.
- Replacement: "a cat that actively hunts mice and other rodents up to monthly (ESCCAP: 4–12 times a year), because rodents carry the cat tapeworm."

**9. "dogs that eat ... faeces" as a Group C/D criterion**
- Current (dogoften.text): "Hunting dogs, dogs that roam freely, dogs that eat prey, carcasses or faeces, and dogs that travel are treated 4–12 times a year"
- Problem: coprophagy is not an ESCCAP Fig. 1 risk criterion (it appears only as a source of false-positive egg counts, line 1755–1757); the Fig. 1 criteria are prey, offal, raw meat, slugs/snails/grass.
- Replacement: "Hunting dogs, dogs that roam freely, dogs that eat prey, carcasses or offal, and dogs that travel are treated 4–12 times a year"

**10. Kitten "otherwise by faecal sample"**
- Current (kitten.text): "...then — for kittens with ongoing risk, such as outdoor access — monthly until 6 months of age, otherwise by faecal sample."
- Problem: ESCCAP says nothing about faecal testing of low-risk kittens between weaning+2 weeks and 6 months; the phrase is an unsourced, slightly awkward add-on.
- Replacement: "...then — for kittens with ongoing risk, such as outdoor access — monthly until 6 months of age; a low-risk kitten needs no further routine treatments after that."

**11. Raw freshwater fish not named as a tapeworm source (Finland-specific, report #37)**
- Current (parasites.text): "Tapeworms occur mainly in animals that eat rodents or raw meat."
- Source: drontal.fi ("raaoista järvikaloista lapamadon eli leveän heisimadon"); Pullola 2006 D. latum 0.4 %.
- Replacement: "Tapeworms occur mainly in animals that eat rodents, raw meat or raw freshwater fish."

**12. Raw-meat freezing specification**
- Current (dogoften.text): "Dogs fed raw meat that has not been frozen or thoroughly heated should also get a monthly tapeworm treatment."
- Source: ESCCAP line 389–390 ("frozen (one week, -17 to -20ºC)").
- Replacement: "Dogs fed raw meat that has not been frozen for at least a week at −17 to −20 °C or thoroughly cooked should also get a monthly tapeworm treatment."
