# Fact-check: kidney_en_master.json (CKD in dogs and cats) — 29-08-2026

Method: every checkable claim was tested against primary/authoritative sources (IRIS 2026 staging + treatment PDFs downloaded from iris-kidney.com; ISFM 2016 consensus full text; Boyd 2008, O'Neill 2013, Rudinsky 2018, Parker 2011, Conroy 2019, Elliott 2000, Ross 2006, Jacob 2002, Plantinga 2005, Hall 2014/2016, Marino 2014, Freeman 2016 abstracts via Europe PMC; IDEXX SDMA pages; Merck Veterinary Manual; ACVIM 2018 hypertension consensus; Semintra EU SPC). Verdicts: VERIFIED / IMPRECISE / WRONG / UNVERIFIABLE. Source list with URLs at the end.

## Five most serious findings (summary)

1. Terminology/prevalence: "30–40 % of cats over 10 have some degree of kidney failure" — the 30–40 % figure is ISFM's CKD prevalence (mostly IRIS stage 1–2, non-azotaemic, symptom-free). Calling it "kidney failure" is wrong and contradicts the article's own stage-1 description.
2. Renal-diet transition "over 1–2 weeks" is WRONG: ISFM says several (e.g. 4–8) weeks; IRIS/nutrition sources say 2–4 weeks minimum. The article also omits the key safety rule: never switch diet while the animal is ill/hospitalised (food aversion).
3. Feline stage-3 survival "typically 1–2 years" understates Boyd 2008 (median 778 days ≈ 2.1 years); stage 2 median was 1,151 days (≈3.2 y) and stage 4 103 days. "Averages" should be "medians". Dog figures match Rudinsky 2018 but rest on 27 dogs; O'Neill 2013 overall median is only 226 days.
4. "Roughly twice as long" on a renal diet: Elliott 2000 = 633 vs 264 days (2.4×), Plantinga 2005 = 16 vs 7 months (2.3×), both non-randomised; the only RCT (Ross 2006) reports 0 % vs 26 % uraemic crises and fewer renal deaths, no survival multiple. Needs honest framing. Dogs (Jacob 2002): 594 vs 188 days — "similar" is actually understated.
5. Drug/treatment precision: "erythropoietin" (human product, off-label; darbepoetin is the recommended form; PRCA risk), "dental disease … accelerates kidney damage" (association only; ISFM: no clear risk factors other than age), telmisartan is EU-licensed for cats only, stage-3 re-check interval should be 2–3 months not 3–6, and "diagnosis within the same visit" overpromises (IRIS requires a stable, hydrated patient, ideally two measurements).

## Internal inconsistencies

- Intro says 30–40 % of cats >10 have "kidney failure" while the stages section defines stage 1 as "kidney damage without symptoms, creatinine normal" — the prevalence figure is dominated by exactly those non-failing stages.
- Stages section: "The stage determines … how often we re-check"; monitoring section then gives one interval for stages 2 and 3 combined.
- Stage 2 is described with "mild signs such as increased drinking", yet the symptoms section says owners notice signs only at two-thirds loss (≈ stage 2–3 boundary) — consistent with IRIS only if stage 2 is described as "usually no symptoms".
- Prognosis: "averages" vs figures that are medians; and "with a renal diet … frequently outlive them" although the Boyd cohort was already largely diet-treated.
- Diet: "the single most effective treatment" yet the transition advice (1–2 weeks, no warning about sick/hospitalised animals) is the part most likely to make the diet fail.

## Safety / overstatement flags for a Finnish vet reading it

1. "Kidney failure" applied to 30–40 % of cats >10 is alarmist and clinically inaccurate (munuaisten vajaatoiminta ≠ IRIS stage 1–2 CKD).
2. Home subcutaneous fluids presented as routine; ISFM: case-by-case, monitor for overhydration; consider cardiac status. Add "for selected patients, under our guidance".
3. "Erythropoietin" — human product, off-label, PRCA and hypertension risks; darbepoetin is the recommended agent. Avoid implying it is a standard, safe step.
4. Telmisartan in dogs and benazepril for canine CKD are off-label in the EU (Semintra licensed for cats only); article need not say so, but should not imply a licensed dog product.
5. ACE inhibitors/ARBs must not be started in a dehydrated or unstable patient (IRIS) — the treatment paragraph could add "once the patient is stable".
6. 1–2-week diet switch and no "never when sick/hospitalised" warning — real risk of learned food aversion.
7. "Seizures" in stage 4 and end-stage — uncommon; frightening for owners without adding value.
8. "Within the same visit" diagnosis — sets up an expectation IRIS explicitly warns against (single raised value ≠ CKD).
9. Survival numbers: cat stage 3 understated; dog per-stage numbers come from a 27-dog study — consider saying "small studies suggest".
10. "Roughly twice as long" should be framed as observational evidence (diet-accepting vs non-accepting cats), not as a randomised result.
